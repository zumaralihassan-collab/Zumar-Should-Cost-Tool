⚖️ Make-vs-Buy Decision Engine

Breakeven-volume analysis that tells you whether to manufacture in-house or outsource — factoring cost, quality and strategic control.

In-house fixed
Annualised CapEx + facility + tooling to stand up production internally.
In-house variable
Material + labour + energy + scrap, per unit you make yourself.
Supplier price
All-in landed price per unit if you outsource the part.
Breakeven
Q* = Fixed ÷ (Buy − Variable). Above it, Make wins on cost.

Selected Portfolio · NPV vs CapEx

Net Present Value (NPV)
Sum of all cash flows discounted back to today at the cost of capital. Rule: NPV > 0 → accept (project creates value). Negative NPV destroys shareholder value even if the project looks profitable on a cash basis. Always project's primary screening metric.
Internal Rate of Return (IRR)
The discount rate at which NPV = 0. Compare to WACC: IRR > WACC → accept. Easy to communicate to finance/exec. Pitfall: assumes reinvestment of intermediate cash flows at the IRR itself (often unrealistic) and breaks down with non-conventional cash flows (multiple sign changes).
Modified IRR (MIRR)
Fixes the IRR reinvestment assumption by assuming positive cash flows are reinvested at the cost of capital (not IRR). More realistic for capital budgeting. Decision rule: same as IRR — compare to WACC.
Payback Period
Years to recover the initial CapEx from undiscounted cash flows. Simple but ignores time value of money & cash flows after payback. Use as a liquidity filter, not the primary decision metric.
Discounted Payback
Same as Payback but uses discounted cash flows. Better than plain Payback; still ignores post-payback cash flows.
Profitability Index (PI)
PI = (PV of future cash flows) / Initial CapEx. PI > 1.0 means each $1 of CapEx returns more than $1 in present value. Critical for capital rationing — when budget is constrained, rank projects by PI to maximize total NPV per dollar of CapEx.
Equivalent Annual Cost (EAC)
Spreads NPV across the project life as an equivalent constant annuity. Essential when comparing assets with different useful lives (e.g. 5-yr machine vs 10-yr machine) — NPV alone is misleading there.
Return on Investment (ROI)
Total undiscounted gain ÷ initial CapEx, expressed as %. Easy to communicate but ignores time value of money and the timing of cash flows. Acceptable as a back-of-envelope screen, never as a final decision metric.
CapEx vs OpEx
CapEx = capitalized one-time spend on assets > useful-life threshold (typically >1 yr, > $1k–5k); depreciated over life. OpEx = ongoing operating expenditure, fully expensed in the year incurred. CapEx affects balance sheet + P&L via depreciation; OpEx hits P&L directly. Procurement decisions often involve a CapEx-vs-OpEx tradeoff (buy vs lease vs subscribe).
CapEx Scope categories
Growth (capacity expansion, new products) · Maintenance (sustaining existing capability) · Replacement (like-for-like end-of-life) · Regulatory / Compliance (mandated, no NPV — risk-adjusted) · Cost-out (savings-driven). Finance teams allocate annual CapEx budget across these buckets with different hurdle rates.
Depreciation tax shield
Depreciation reduces taxable income → reduces tax → effectively returns cash. Tax shield = Depreciation × Tax rate. MACRS (Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System) is the US tax depreciation schedule — front-loaded, increases early-year cash and NPV vs straight-line.
WACC — Weighted Avg Cost of Capital
The blended cost of debt + equity, used as the discount rate / hurdle rate. Typical WACC: 7–12% for industrial firms. Always discount with after-tax WACC for after-tax cash flows. Project-specific risk may justify a premium above corporate WACC.

📚 About these models

Simple Exponential Smoothing (SES) — Flat-trend, fast-adapting; α weights recent observations more heavily.

Holt's Linear Trend — Adds a trend component; great when demand is steadily growing or declining.

Holt-Winters Triple Exp — Adds seasonality; ideal for SKUs with quarterly or annual cycles.

AR(1) ARIMA — Autoregressive component captures momentum; the workhorse for stable-demand items.

⚖️ Quote Comparison

Score up to four supplier quotes side-by-side against your should-cost — price variance, a weighted decision score across price, quality, lead-time and risk, and an auto-ranked award recommendation.

Should-cost anchor
Every quote is measured as a % variance against your calculated should-cost — your objective baseline.
Weighted score
Price, quality, lead-time and risk are normalised 0-100 and blended by your weights (auto-summed to 100%).
Relative scoring
Price & lead-time score best-in-set; quality is higher-is-better; risk is lower-is-better.
Award, not sticker
The highest weighted score is recommended — the best total value, not just the cheapest price.
